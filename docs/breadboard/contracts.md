# Breadboard Lab — Working Contracts
**Owner:** Lead (as of 2026-05-18)
**Purpose:** This is the single source of truth for how the Breadboard View + Lab must behave. All implementation, refactoring, and testing decisions must be justified against these rules.
**Source:** Consolidated from `pp-view-breadboard/references/*` and `breadboard-lab/references/*` + lessons from the 2026-05-18 deep analysis.

---

## 1. Core Identity

The Breadboard tab must feel like **one continuous, realistic maker workbench**, not a collection of features.

A user should be able to:
- Open a project
- See parts on a bench / stash
- Place real or starter parts
- Wire them
- Get useful guidance
- Inspect the actual hardware they built
- Move cleanly into schematic, validation, inventory, or procurement

Everything else is secondary to this feeling.

---

## 2. Provenance & Trust (Highest Priority)

Users must be able to instantly tell the difference between:

- **Starter / generic** parts (low trust, suggested)
- **Project-linked** parts (from inventory or previous work)
- **Bench / stash** parts (physically present on the bench but not yet placed)
- **Exact / verified** parts (user confirmed or hardware-inspected)
- **Coach-generated** suggestions
- **Synced** from schematic (engine-generated)

**Rules:**
- Every instance and wire must carry clear, machine-readable provenance at creation time.
- Visual treatment (borders, icons, badges, line style) must make provenance obvious at a glance without requiring the inspector.
- Coach output must **never** appear more trusted than verified or user-placed data.
- When a part or wire is synced from another view, the user must be able to distinguish it from hand-drawn work.

**Failure mode:** A beginner cannot tell what is "real" vs "suggested" within 5–10 seconds.

---

## 3. Bench vs Board Model (Core Lab Distinction)

The "Lab" concept exists because parts can live in two states:

- **On the bench / in stash** (benchX/benchY set, breadboardX/breadboardY null)
- **Placed on the breadboard** (breadboard coordinates set)

**Rules:**
- The system must clearly distinguish these two states in both data and UI.
- Wiring to bench-placed parts (via connector anchors) must be supported and obvious.
- Moving a part from bench to board (or vice versa) should feel like a deliberate, understandable action.
- Coordinates must not silently conflict or produce spooky behavior.

**Failure mode:** Users are confused about whether a part is "on the bench" or "on the board."

---

## 4. Coach, AI Guidance, and Board Health

Guidance must be **actionable and trustworthy**, never magical.

**Rules:**
- Coach plans and board health issues must be visible and reachable from the main workbench without hunting.
- Applying a coach suggestion or focusing a health issue must update the visual state and provenance appropriately.
- A coach remediation must result in something the user can clearly see was "suggested" (not presented as verified).
- Board health output must be tied to specific parts/pins and offer clear next actions.
- Hardware inspection results should feed into the same trust model.

**Failure mode:** The coach feels like a black box or produces output that looks more authoritative than actual hardware data.

---

## 5. Schematic ↔ Breadboard Sync

Sync is the highest-risk operation in the entire Lab.

**Rules (from ai-audit-and-sync.md):**
- Silent success is dangerous. The user must be able to tell whether a wire was hand-drawn or generated by sync.
- Selecting a synced part must still expose the correct trust and readiness state.
- Sync must not create duplicate wires or destroy user intent.
- Provenance must be preserved and visually distinguishable after sync.

**Failure mode:** The user loses confidence that they understand what actually exists on the bench.

---

## 6. UI / Layout / Discoverability (Workbench Feel)

**Rules:**
- Primary actions must stay close to the work surface.
- Secondary actions belong in scrollable menus or collapsible panels.
- Hardware inspection must be reachable without hunting.
- The page must not depend on huge fixed viewport heights.
- Empty states must offer a real next step, not just explanation.
- Dangerous or uncertain data must be visibly marked.
- Toolbars should prefer grouped actions over repeated one-off buttons.

**Failure mode:** The UI feels like a pile of features instead of a workbench.

---

## 7. Realism & Physical Intuition

The Lab must compete with (and ideally beat) dedicated breadboard tools on physical feel for the education and hobbyist personas.

**Rules:**
- Placement, leg bending, collision, and wiring behavior should feel physically plausible.
- The bench area should support the same intuition as the actual board.
- Hardware photo inspection should feel like a natural extension of working on the bench, not a separate tool.

**Current known gap:** Systematic validation against Fritzing-level physical realism is weak.

---

## 8. Testing & Verification

**Mandatory practices:**
- Follow the testing guide in `pp-view-breadboard/references/testing.md`
- Every visible behavior change requires both test updates **and** real browser verification.
- Warnings count as defects. Do not ship with known warnings in the breadboard flow.
- Run both `inspect-breadboard-view.mjs` and the breadboard-lab inspector before major changes.
- Contract guard tests must exist and must fail if the core rules above are violated.

---

## 9. What "Good" Looks Like (Definition of Done for Major Waves)

After the first-wave work (especially canvas extraction + provenance):

- A new user can open Breadboard on a mixed project and correctly identify the origin and trust level of parts within 10 seconds.
- The 1,677 LOC canvas has been broken into focused, owned modules with clear boundaries.
- Coach and board health output are first-class, immediately useful parts of the workbench.
- Hardware inspection feels like a natural part of the bench workflow.
- Sync no longer feels spooky.

---

## 10. Change Process

Any code change that affects:
- Provenance
- Bench vs board state
- Sync behavior
- Coach presentation or application
- Trust/readiness visuals

...must be reviewed against this document before merging.

This contracts file is the spec. The skill references are the source material. When they drift, this document wins until the skills are updated.
