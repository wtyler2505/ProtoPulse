# ARCANA — BUSINESS AND MARKETING PLAYBOOK

Title: Arcana Business and Marketing Playbook  
Purpose: Help Arcana turn customer problems into focused offers, credible business models, differentiated positioning, practical distribution, ethical marketing, and low-cost market tests.  
Version: 1.0  
Last Updated: 2026-06-21  
Intended Uses: New ventures, small businesses, services, products, nonprofits, creators, positioning, customer research, pricing, distribution, retention, campaigns, partnerships, sales, and strategic growth.  
Not Intended For: Inventing market evidence, guaranteeing revenue, replacing legal or financial review, manipulating audiences, or treating attention as proof of value.  
Evidence Standard: Business recommendations should distinguish sourced facts, observed behavior, assumptions, and creative inference. Current competitors, prices, laws, channels, platform rules, and market conditions should be verified before consequential use.

---

# HOW ARCANA SHOULD USE THIS PLAYBOOK

Arcana should begin with the customer’s situation and the user’s desired business, not with a channel, campaign, or technology.

The default sequence is:

1. Define the customer, triggering situation, and desired progress.
2. Identify the current alternative and cost of doing nothing.
3. Select a narrow entry point.
4. Design a clear offer around one mechanism.
5. Choose a distribution route consistent with audience behavior.
6. Test demand through meaningful commitment.
7. Map delivery, economics, trust, and retention.
8. Recommend one direction and one immediate test.

Do not respond to “How do I grow?” with a generic list of social media, email, SEO, partnerships, and content.

Growth advice must specify:

- Who acts
- In what situation
- Because of what promise
- Through which route
- With what proof
- Toward which behavior
- At what operational cost
- With what evidence of repeat value

# CUSTOMER PROBLEM DEFINITION

## Situation Before Segment

Demographics rarely explain why someone acts now.

Define:

- Triggering event
- Desired progress
- Current workaround
- Cost of current state
- Urgency
- Decision process
- Trust requirements
- Constraints
- Evidence of success

## Job Statement

> When ___ happens, [customer] wants to ___, so they can ___.

Example:

> When a first-time manager prepares for a difficult performance conversation, they want a clear structure and language they can trust, so they can address the issue without damaging the relationship.

## Problem Quality Test

A strong problem is:

- Specific
- Repeated or consequential
- Already producing a workaround
- Connected to action
- Experienced by an identifiable audience
- Possible to serve
- Worth solving under the user’s goals

## Current Alternatives

Competition includes:

- Doing nothing
- Delaying
- Hiring someone
- Using a spreadsheet
- Asking a friend
- Combining several tools
- Accepting the problem
- Changing the goal

A product must beat the customer’s real alternative, not only a named competitor.

# AUDIENCE AND WEDGE

## Wedge Definition

A wedge is a narrow audience and moment where the need is intense enough to support action.

Define:

- Specific audience
- Trigger
- Urgency
- Existing alternative
- Reachable channel
- Meaningful commitment
- Expansion path

## Wedge Quality Questions

- Does the audience recognize the problem?
- Does the trigger create urgency?
- Can they be reached without building a new audience first?
- Is the offer easy to explain?
- Can the first version be delivered?
- Does serving them produce useful learning?
- Is there a plausible adjacent audience?

## Weak Wedges

- “Small businesses”
- “Busy people”
- “Anyone who wants to improve”
- “Creators”
- “Everyone in the local area”

## Stronger Wedges

- Independent cafés opening a second location
- First-time managers preparing for formal reviews
- Parents organizing a child’s first allergy-safe birthday
- Freelance designers with overdue client approvals
- Neighborhood bookstores trying to increase second visits without discounts

# CUSTOMER RESEARCH

## Research Goals

Use research to understand:

- Triggering situations
- Existing behavior
- Workarounds
- Language
- Anxiety
- Decision criteria
- Trust
- Switching cost
- Meaningful outcomes

Do not use interviews primarily to collect compliments.

## Interview Questions

- Tell me about the last time this happened.
- What did you do first?
- What made that difficult?
- What did you try?
- What did it cost in time, money, effort, or risk?
- Who else was involved?
- What almost stopped you?
- What would a good outcome have looked like?
- What happened afterward?
- What do you do now?

## Avoid

- “Would you use this?”
- “Do you like this idea?”
- “How much would you pay?” without context
- Leading questions
- Explaining the solution before understanding behavior
- Treating stated intention as demand

# OFFER DESIGN

An offer combines:

- Audience
- Trigger
- Outcome
- Mechanism
- Scope
- Proof
- Price or exchange
- Risk reduction
- Next action

## Offer Formula

> For [specific audience] facing [specific situation], [offer] helps them achieve [outcome] through [mechanism], without [major objection or burden].

## Offer Quality

A strong offer is:

- Specific
- Understandable
- Credible
- Bounded
- Actionable
- Easy to compare with doing nothing
- Deliverable
- Connected to a real next step

## Scope

State:

- What is included
- What is excluded
- Duration
- Deliverables
- Customer responsibilities
- Support
- Revision or recovery
- Completion condition

## Risk Reduction

Use:

- Trial
- Pilot
- Clear guarantee
- Milestones
- Reversible commitment
- Sample
- Demonstration
- Transparent cancellation
- Limited scope
- Proof from comparable users

Do not hide risk with inflated promises.

## Positioning Frame


## Category Choice

The category sets expectations.

Possible moves:

- Enter an established category
- Narrow a category
- Combine two categories
- Reframe the problem
- Create a new category only when necessary

New categories require education and distribution effort. Do not invent one merely to sound distinctive.

## Differentiation Mechanisms

- Different audience
- Different trigger
- Different delivery
- Different risk allocation
- Different business model
- Different participation role
- Different speed
- Different evidence
- Different lifecycle
- Different constraint

Cosmetic differentiation includes:

- New adjectives
- Trendy visual style
- Generic personalization claims
- A feature competitors can copy immediately
- “Premium” without a changed experience

## Positioning Stress Test

Ask target customers:

- What do you think this is?
- Who is it for?
- What problem does it solve?
- What would you compare it with?
- What makes you skeptical?
- What would make it worth acting on?

# UNIT ECONOMICS AND CAPACITY

Arcana should use simple, transparent reasoning and avoid false precision.

## Basic Questions

- Revenue per customer or transaction
- Direct delivery cost
- Labor time
- Support cost
- Refund or failure cost
- Acquisition cost
- Retention or repeat rate
- Capacity constraint
- Cash timing

## Contribution Logic

> Contribution before overhead = revenue minus direct delivery and service costs.

Do not call a model viable because gross revenue is positive.

- Clearer invitation

- Physical sample
- Simplified form
- Better timing

## Smart Bet

Create a recurring participation format.

Examples:

- Challenge
- Series
- Passport
- Office hours
- Rotating host
- Customer showcase
- Referral ritual

## Paradigm Shift

Change the audience’s role.

Examples:

- Customer becomes curator

## Deepen

Serve the current audience better.

## Broaden

Serve an adjacent audience.

## Extend

Solve a problem before or after the current offer.

## Bundle

Combine complementary value.

## Unbundle

Separate a valuable part from a bloated offer.

## Partner

Reach or serve through another organization.

## License

Allow another party to deliver the mechanism.

Turn repeated service work into a standardized offer.

Increase value, proof, service, or risk reduction—not merely price.

# REAL-WORLD-STYLE APPLICATIONS

These examples are illustrative and not verified market claims.

## Example 1 — Local Café Retention

### Request

A café wants repeat visits without discounting.

### Diagnosis

The existing visit ends completely. Customers have no unfinished value or visible participation.

### Recommendation

Create a six-week neighborhood tasting map. Each visit unlocks one local ingredient story and lets the customer leave a pairing suggestion displayed the following week.

### Mechanism

- Unfinished loop
- Visible handprint
- Participation
- Local identity

### Test

Print 30 maps and run two weeks. Pass if at least nine participants make a second visit and staff explanation remains below one minute.

---

## Example 2 — Freelance Service Positioning

### Request

A designer offers “branding, websites, and marketing” but struggles to close clients.

### Diagnosis

The offer is organized around capabilities rather than a triggering customer situation.

### Wedge

Independent hospitality businesses opening a second location.

### Offer

A two-week “Second Location Brand Transfer” that documents the brand, adapts essential customer touchpoints, and creates a launch kit for new staff.

### Test

Send ten highly relevant, personalized invitations to qualified businesses. Seek three calls and one paid pilot.

---

## Example 3 — Creator Membership

### Request

A creator wants recurring revenue.

### Diagnosis

The content itself may not produce recurring value. A subscription is premature.

### Recommendation

Run a four-week paid cohort centered on completing one real project. Use the cohort to discover which ongoing support creates repeat value.

### Pass

At least 40 percent of participants request or commit to a clearly defined continuation after the project ends.

### Stop

Do not launch an indefinite membership if continuation interest depends mainly on access to the creator rather than a scalable mechanism.

- The customer is specific.
- The trigger and current alternative are visible.
- The problem is not inferred only from demographics.
- The offer contains a mechanism.
- The wedge is reachable.
- Positioning is more than adjectives.
- Distribution fits audience behavior.
- Demand is tested through meaningful commitment.
- Pricing is connected to value and delivery.
- Operational burden is included.
- Growth does not automatically count as success.
- Retention reflects repeated value.
- Ethical persuasion and easy exit are preserved.
- Claims are proportional to evidence.
- Current market facts are verified when necessary.

# MAINTENANCE RULES

1. Verify changing competitors, prices, channel rules, laws, and market conditions.
2. Keep foundational strategy separate from volatile channel tactics.
3. Remove generic growth advice.
4. Add examples only when the mechanism and limitations are clear.
5. Do not let famous companies substitute for evidence.
6. Preserve low-cost, low-technology, and manual tests.
7. Treat customer behavior as stronger evidence than compliments.
8. Include operational and ethical costs.
9. Revisit the wedge before expanding.
10. Prefer sustainable value over attention.
